3D printed customised air pistol grip

For the past 9 years that I have shot air pistol, the grips have always been ill fitting. Even with the smallest standard grips, either it was too long or too thick, especially since my fingers are relatively small. So when I built my Voron 0.2, my first 3d printer, the first thing I printed on it after the callibration prints was a customised grip that was better fit for myself.

Of course, being an Olympic sport, there are rules about what the grip can or cannot be shaped like, dictated by the rule book from the International Sport Shooting Federation (ISSF). I read through the rules, took note of the resitrictions on the grips and got to designing my own grip. Unfortunately I could not use some other more powerful/more convenient photogrammetry tools because they were built for CUDA, and I did not possess a computer with an Nvidia GPU. Therefore I used the open source photogrammetry tools COLMAP and openMVS to scan and reconstruct my previously best fitting grip, from which I would tweak to fit my hand.

Of course there were setbacks initially. The output of photogrammetry is a point cloud or a mesh file, both of which traditional CAD software cannot process. However, I did have some previous experience with Blender, a 3d mesh modelling software normally used for animation, which led me to try to model the grip in Blender. However, the lack of constraints and real measurements were limiting factors in modelling an accurate grip suitable for mating with a pistol. Luckily, Autodesk Inventor allows importing meshes as reference geometry, and had powerful surfacing tools, which are both needed to reconstruct the grip. I used Blender to make a simplified mesh model of the curved surfaces, which I imported into Inventor

I used Inventor's freeform modelling capabilities to carefully reconstruct the the complex curves present on the grip. After the CAD model roughly matched the scanned grip, I added the constraints from the rules, then printed a first prototype. As expected it was too big to fit my hand comfortably. I then adjusted the grip in the places that were too big and printed it again. I adjusted and printed again and again until the grip perfectly hugged my hand. Having a 3d printer available definitely helped lower the iteration time considerably, and showed me the utility of rapid prototyping machines in manufacturing and design.
